# GO
## Official Game Rule Book

**Version:** 1.0  
**Game:** Go (囲碁)  
**Genre:** Strategy / Board Game  
**Players:** 2 (Black and White)

---

# 1. Introduction

Go is an ancient two-player strategy board game that originated in China. It is played using black and white stones on a grid-shaped board.

The objective is to control more territory than your opponent by strategically placing stones, surrounding empty spaces, and capturing opposing stones.

Go is a game of strategy, patience, observation, and tactical thinking.

# 2. Game Components

A standard Go game consists of:

- **Go Board:** A grid of 19 × 19 lines.
- **Black Stones:** Used by the first player.
- **White Stones:** Used by the second player.
- **Players:** Two participants.
- **Computer Opponent:** Available in single-player versions.

### Board Sizes

Go can be played on different board sizes:

| Board Size | Recommended For |
|---|---|
| 9 × 9 | Beginners |
| 13 × 13 | Intermediate |
| 19 × 19 | Standard competitive play |

# 3. Objective of the Game

The main objective is to acquire more territory than your opponent.

Players achieve this by:

1. Occupying strategic positions.
2. Surrounding empty intersections.
3. Capturing opponent stones.
4. Protecting their own groups.
5. Building larger territorial areas.

The player with the higher final score wins.

# 4. Starting the Game

1. The game begins with an empty board.
2. Black always plays first.
3. White plays second.
4. Players alternate turns.
5. A stone must be placed on an empty intersection.
6. Once placed, a stone cannot be moved voluntarily.
7. Players continue until both agree to end the game by passing consecutively.

# 5. Basic Gameplay Rules

## Rule 1: Placing Stones

- Players place one stone per turn.
- Stones are placed at the intersections of the grid lines.
- A stone cannot be placed on an already occupied intersection.
- Players cannot move their stones after placement.

## Rule 2: Liberties

A liberty is an empty intersection directly connected horizontally or vertically to a stone.

Diagonal intersections do not count as liberties.

Every stone or connected group must have at least one liberty to remain on the board.

## Rule 3: Capturing Stones

A stone or connected group is captured when all its liberties are occupied by the opponent.

When a group has zero liberties, it is immediately removed from the board.

## Rule 4: Connected Groups

Stones of the same colour that are connected horizontally or vertically form a group.

All stones in a connected group share their available liberties.

## Rule 5: The Ko Rule

The Ko rule prevents players from repeatedly recreating the same board position through immediate recapture.

A player cannot make a move that immediately recreates the previous board position.

## Rule 6: Suicide Rule

A player cannot place a stone in a position where it would have no liberties, unless that move captures opposing stones and creates liberties.

## Rule 7: Passing

A player may pass instead of placing a stone.

When both players pass consecutively, the game ends and scoring begins.

# 6. Special Concepts

## Eyes

An eye is an empty intersection surrounded by stones of the same colour.

A group with two genuine eyes is generally considered alive because the opponent cannot capture it.

## Territory

Territory consists of empty intersections surrounded by stones of one colour.

## Seki

Seki is a shared-life situation in which opposing groups coexist without either side being able to capture the other safely.

## Dead Stones

Dead stones are stones or groups that cannot avoid capture under correct play.

They are removed during the scoring process if both players agree they are dead.

# 7. Scoring System

Go has multiple scoring systems. This game uses the **Territory Scoring System**.

The final score is calculated using:

**Final Score = Territory Points + Captured Stones + Komi**

- Territory Points: Empty intersections surrounded by the player's stones.
- Captured Stones: Opponent stones removed during the game.
- Komi: Bonus points awarded to White to compensate for Black playing first.

### Komi

A standard modern value is 6.5 points, although the value depends on the ruleset.

The half-point prevents a tied score.

# 8. Winning Conditions

The game ends when both players pass consecutively.

After the game ends:

1. Players identify dead stones.
2. Dead stones are removed.
3. Territory is counted.
4. Captured stones are included.
5. Komi is added to White's score.
6. Final scores are compared.

The player with the highest score wins.

# 9. Illegal Moves

The following moves are prohibited:

- Placing a stone on an occupied intersection.
- Making an illegal suicide move.
- Repeating a prohibited Ko position.
- Making a move outside the board.
- Moving or removing an already placed stone voluntarily.

Illegal moves should not change the game state.

# 10. Game Etiquette

Players are expected to:

- Respect their opponent.
- Play one move at a time.
- Avoid disturbing stones already placed.
- Think carefully before making a move.
- Accept the final score.
- Maintain fair and respectful gameplay.

# 11. Beginner Strategy Tips

1. **Control the Corners:** Corners are easier to surround than edges and central areas.
2. **Secure the Sides:** Build territory along the board edges.
3. **Protect Your Groups:** Avoid leaving groups without sufficient liberties.
4. **Create Two Eyes:** Two genuine eyes help ensure a group survives.
5. **Think Ahead:** Consider your opponent's possible responses.
6. **Balance Attack and Defence:** Do not focus entirely on capturing stones.
7. **Use Influence:** Build strong positions that can expand into larger territories.

# 13. Final Rule

The most important principle of Go is:

**Control territory, protect your stones, capture when necessary, and think strategically.**

Go is not simply about capturing your opponent's stones. It is about creating influence, controlling space, and making better strategic decisions.
